# Laplace-law verification of the CSF surface-tension model

**Status: FAIL (honest negative result).** This document records the first
quantitative comparison of the surface-tension model shipped in
`tensorsph.surface_tension` with the exact equilibrium condition for a curved
interface. The model does **not** satisfy it, and its error grows under
refinement. Nothing here is a claim about stability — the solver stays stable
with `sigma > 0`; the claim is that the force has no convergent physical value.

Reproduce:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python benchmarks/surface_tension/laplace.py \
    --radii-dx 16,32,64,96 --out benchmarks/surface_tension/laplace_ladder.json
PYTHONPATH=src python -m pytest tests/test_surface_tension_laplace.py -v
```

Every number below is read from `benchmarks/surface_tension/laplace_ladder.json`
and the figure is generated by the same run; none is transcribed by hand.

---

## 1. What is tested, and why it matters

The README declares "surface tension model (CSF method, Brackbill et al. 1992)
... configurable via `Config.surface_tension_coefficient`", and
`benchmarks/static_droplet` checks that the force points inward and that its
magnitude is "physically reasonable". Neither statement tests a physical law: a
force can point inward and be off by an order of magnitude.

The exact law available without any experiment is the two-dimensional **Laplace
law**: for a circular interface of radius `R` in equilibrium,

    Delta p = sigma / R.

At `sigma = 0.072 N/m` (water-air at 20 C) and `R = 0.1 m`, the target jump is
**0.7200 Pa** at every resolution. A model that cannot
reproduce a constant under refinement is not a discretisation of that law, and
no capillary-driven simulation (droplet oscillation, breakup, capillary wave)
can be trusted at any resolution until it does.

## 2. Method

The CSF force is a volumetric density

    f = -sigma * kappa * n * delta_s,   delta_s = |grad c|,   (N/m^3)

and the Laplace law is its normal integral,
`integral f . (-n) dxi = sigma * kappa = sigma / R`. The measurement must
therefore integrate across the interface and must **not** compare a pointwise
acceleration with `sigma/(rho R)`: the pointwise field carries the
resolution-dependent factor `delta_s`. The shipped
`benchmarks/static_droplet/validation_report.json` compares
`expected_acceleration_scale_m_per_s2 = 0.00072` with a measured
`max_acceleration_m_per_s2`, i.e. it compares quantities of different
dimensions; that comparison cannot detect the defect reported here.

**Estimator.** For a radially symmetric field the identity
`integral f dr = (1/2 pi) integral f dA / r` is evaluated with the
force-weighted centroid radius `r_c`, so

    Delta p = sum_i rho_i (-a_i . n_i) dx^2 / (2 pi r_c).

`n` is the *exact* geometric outward normal of the disc (the model's own normal
is a separate object of study), and the acceleration `a` is whatever the module
returns. `r_c` removes the O(t/R) bias a plain `/2 pi R` normalisation would
introduce; without it the coarse levels show a spurious 20-60% offset.

**Gauge self-test.** The same estimator is applied to a synthetic smooth bump
field whose normal integral is exactly `sigma/R`. Its residual
(`synthetic_recovery_error`, below) is the measurement error of this benchmark.
It never exceeds 0.1%, i.e. the
measurement is far more accurate than the smallest model error reported. Every
failure below is therefore attributable to the model, not to the gauge.

**Ablation.** Two runs replace exactly one ingredient of the model with its
exact counterpart, using the module's own other ingredient:
(i) exact curvature `kappa = 1/R` with the model's `delta_s`;
(ii) the model's `kappa` with an exact, normalised one-sided delta function.
This localises the defect instead of only reporting a total error.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Resolution ladder (`sigma = 0.072 N/m`, `R = 0.1 m`, `rho0 = 1000.0 kg/m^3`)

| R/dx | N | dx [m] | Delta p measured [Pa] | ratio to sigma/R | rel. error | gauge error | kappa R (median) | kappa R (force-weighted) | int delta_s | inward frac. | detector recall / precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 797 | 0.00625 | 0.3486 | 0.484 | **51.6%** | 0.1% | -0.210 | -0.000 | 0.253 | 0.935 | 1.00 / 0.96 |
| 32 | 3209 | 0.00313 | 0.7729 | 1.074 | **7.4%** | 0.1% | -0.139 | -0.000 | 0.273 | 0.955 | 0.83 / 0.99 |
| 64 | 12853 | 0.00156 | 1.6559 | 2.300 | **130.0%** | 0.1% | -0.156 | 0.000 | 0.281 | 0.958 | 0.82 / 0.99 |
| 96 | 28917 | 0.00104 | 2.5343 | 3.520 | **252.0%** | 0.0% | -0.256 | 0.000 | 0.284 | 0.960 | 0.86 / 0.99 |

Target value `sigma/R = 0.7200 Pa`; exact curvature in this
convention is `kappa R = +1` and a proper one-sided normalised delta function
integrates to `1`.

Observed order of the Laplace error from the two finest levels:
**-1.63** — negative, i.e. the error *grows* with
refinement. A negative order cannot be cured by refining; the discrete force has
no convergent limit.

### 3.2 Ablation: where the error comes from

| R/dx | Delta p, exact kappa [Pa] | ratio | Delta p, exact delta [Pa] | Delta p, full model [Pa] | ratio | int delta_s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 0.1953 | 0.271 | -0.00000 | 0.3486 | 0.484 | 0.253 |
| 32 | 0.2032 | 0.282 | -0.00000 | 0.7729 | 1.074 | 0.273 |
| 64 | 0.2056 | 0.286 | 0.00000 | 1.6559 | 2.300 | 0.281 |
| 96 | 0.2069 | 0.287 | 0.00000 | 2.5343 | 3.520 | 0.284 |

Reading of the two ablations:

* With **exact curvature** the jump converges to
  `~0.207 Pa`
  (`0.29 x` theory) and is
  stable across the ladder. That residual is exactly `int delta_s`:
  0.253 -> 0.284. The model's
  `delta_s = |grad c|` integrates to ~0.27 instead of 1, so even with perfect
  curvature the force would be ~3.6x too weak. **Defect 1: unnormalised surface
  delta function.**
* With an **exact normalised delta**, the model's own curvature produces
  `~1.2e-13 Pa` — statistically zero at every level.
  The force-weighted `kappa R` column above is `0.000`
  against an exact `+1`, and `50.0%` of interface particles
  carry `kappa > 0`. **Defect 2: the curvature field is zero-mean noise, not
  `1/R`** — it spans the exact value only as noise and cancels in any integral.
* In the **full model** the two noisy fields are built from the same
  neighbour-count colour function and are correlated, so their product produces
  a spurious *inward* force whose magnitude grows as `dx` shrinks
  (0.48 -> 3.52 x theory). **Defect 3: the product
  amplifies the shared noise; the apparent inward pull is not curvature.**

The shipped static-droplet benchmark passes precisely because direction and a
"reasonable" pointwise magnitude survive all three defects: 93.5%-96.0%
of detected surface particles are pushed inward at every level.

### 3.3 Why a single resolution is not enough

At `R/dx = 32` the error is only `7.4%`, i.e. the model
would have *passed* a 15% gate; the next refinement gives
`130.0%`. The non-monotone error is itself the signature of
noise dominating a physical term. Any future verification of this model must
report a ladder, and a single-level pass should be treated as uninformative.

## 4. Error judgement against the platform criterion

The platform criterion is |error| <= 3% (with 15% the absolute acceptability
threshold) for key quantities against experiment or an analytical reference.

| quantity | reference | measured | relative error | < 3%? | < 15%? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laplace pressure jump, R/dx = 16 | 0.7200 Pa | 0.3486 Pa | 51.6% | no | no |
| Laplace pressure jump, R/dx = 32 | 0.7200 Pa | 0.7729 Pa | 7.4% | no | yes |
| Laplace pressure jump, R/dx = 64 | 0.7200 Pa | 1.6559 Pa | 130.0% | no | no |
| Laplace pressure jump, R/dx = 96 (finest) | 0.7200 Pa | 2.5343 Pa | 252.0% | no | no |
| curvature `kappa R` (force-weighted), R/dx = 96 | +1 | 0.000 | 100.0% | no | no |
| one-sided delta integral `int delta_s` | 1 | 0.284 | 71.6% | no | no |

**Answer to "is it < 3%?": no.** No level is within 3%; the best level is
7.4% and the finest is 252.0%. The failure is not marginal — it is a
structural absence of a convergent discretisation, and it does not improve with
refinement (observed order -1.63).

## 5. Limitations and scope

* **Two-dimensional, single-phase, static geometry.** The force is evaluated on
  a fixed disc (no time integration), which isolates the *discretisation* of the
  law from dynamics and stability. Dynamics can only add error.
* **Uniform density.** `rho = rho0` everywhere, so the density ratio used as the
  colour function is trivial; this is the most favourable case for the shipped
  model. A real free surface with a density deficit (as in the hydrostatic and
  dam-break cases) can only worsen it.
* **`h/dx = 1.3` (module default) and the default `0.7 * mean`
  surface threshold** were used throughout; no tuning of the model was attempted.
  Interface width `2h/R` ranges from 16.2% at
  `R/dx = 16` to 2.7% at
  `R/dx = 96`; `R/dx = 8` was excluded because the interface width
  there approaches a third of the radius and the thin-interface assumption behind
  the estimator fails.
* **The gauge is a measurement check, not a physics check.** It bounds the
  estimator error at <= 0.1%; it says nothing about the model.
* **Not measured here:** capillary-wave dispersion (`omega^2 = sigma k^3 / rho`),
  droplet oscillation frequency (Lamb), and multi-phase Laplace behaviour. All
  require this defect to be fixed first to be informative.

## 6. Recommended fix (for the follow-up mission)

1. Normalise the surface delta: replace `delta_s = |grad c|` with a one-sided
   distribution whose integral is 1, or absorb the factor into the force, and
   verify `int delta_s = 1 +- 5%` as a unit test on flat and curved interfaces.
2. Replace the curvature estimator: `div(n)` of a normalised gradient over a
   *neighbour-count* colour field is zero-mean noise in this configuration.
   Options verifiable against this benchmark: curvature from a renormalised
   (first-order consistent) gradient of a Shepard-smoothed indicator, or the
   `-lap(c)/|grad c|` form with a consistent Laplacian; both must be validated
   on the ladder in this file, not on a single resolution.
3. Keep this benchmark as the acceptance gate: `|Delta p/(sigma/R) - 1| <= 15%`
   at `R/dx = 32` **and** a non-negative observed order, otherwise a single
   lucky level can masquerade as a pass.
4. Only then re-enable any physical-validation claim for capillary-driven cases.

## Update (mission m2454): the defect is repaired to within the 15 % gate

The interface fields were rebuilt (`interface_fields()` in `tensorsph.surface_tension`):
colour from kernel completeness, one smoothing length `h_c = 2 h` for the whole interface,
first-order-consistent gradient (Cummins & Rudman matrix, singular values clamped), and a
fluid-side-normalised `delta_s = 2 |grad c|`; the neighbour-count surface detector is gone
because `delta_s` already localises the force.

| R/dx | Laplace error, 0.8.0 | Laplace error, now | kappa R, 0.8.0 | kappa R, now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 51.6 % | 2.4 % | -0.210 | 1.367 |
| 32 | 7.4 % (false PASS) | 7.5 % | -0.139 | 1.236 |
| 64 | 130.0 % | 10.8 % | -0.156 | 1.092 |
| 96 | 252.0 % | 12.0 % | -0.256 | 1.111 |

Gates: `laplace_error_finest` and `curvature_sign_and_scale` PASS; `force_direction` still
FAILS (0.97 of the interface particles at `R/dx = 96` are pushed inwards, not 1.00), so the
benchmark verdict remains FAIL. The residual 12 % is resolution-independent (observed order
-0.26) and is the sum of two partially cancelling one-sided errors: `int delta_s = 0.75`
instead of 1, and `kappa R = 1.11`. Do not read the table as convergence.

Note also that the benchmark's inward/curvature statistics are now taken on the interface
layer the model actually acts on (`delta_s > 0.1 max`), not on the old neighbour-count
detector; the detector recall/precision diagnostics are kept for reference only.
