# Verification: plane Poiseuille channel flow (viscous operator and no-slip wall)

**Status: split verdict, measured.** The steady profile is a parabola to 1.2% of the centre-line speed, and at `H/dx = 16` the peak velocity lands 1.5% from the analytic value, inside the 3 % gate. The wall shear, however, is 50% low at *every* resolution and the fluid keeps a slip velocity of 31% of `u_max` at the wall, with the effective no-slip plane about 0.9 spacings outside the boundary. The good peak number is the cancellation of those two defects, not the resolution of the wall layer, so this case does **not** certify no-slip channel flow.

Generated by `benchmarks/poiseuille/make_doc.py` from `benchmarks/poiseuille/out/results.json` (torch 2.7.1, device `cpu`).

## 1. Case

An incompressible fluid of kinematic viscosity `nu` fills a 2-D channel of
height `H` with no-slip walls at `y = 0` and `y = H` and is driven by a uniform
body force `g_x` along `x`. The domain is periodic in `x`, so the problem is
one-dimensional and the steady solution of
`0 = -dp/dx + mu d2u/dy2 + rho g_x` with mass conservation is

    u(y) = g_x (y - a)(b - y) / (2 nu),   a = 0, b = H,

with peak velocity `u_max = g_x H^2 / (8 nu) = 1.2263 m/s` and wall
shear `tau_w = rho g_x H / 2 = 490.5 Pa`. The
steady state is reached by starting from rest and integrating long enough
(`t_end = 1.0 s`, about 9.9 viscous
time scales `H^2/(pi^2 nu)`).

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| channel height `H` | 0.1 m |
| channel length | 12 fluid columns (`columns * dx`) |
| kinematic viscosity `nu` | 0.01 m^2/s (Re = O(12)) |
| body force `g_x` | 9.81 m/s^2 |
| sound speed `c0` | 20 m/s, `h = 1.3 dx`, cubic spline kernel |
| wall model | Adami et al. (2012) pressure extrapolation, `wall_layers = 3`, `wall_friction = 1` |
| x boundary | periodic (two image copies of the fluid cloud) |
| averaging window | last 0.2 s of the run, per-row mean over the columns |

## 2. Method

1. The fluid is a square lattice of `rho0 dx^2` masses; wall particles sit one
   spacing outside the first fluid row, three layers deep, and are extended
   three spacings past both periodic ends.
2. The step is `vel += dt * (a_pressure + a_viscosity + g_x e_x)` with a fixed
   `dt = min(0.3 h / c0, 0.125 h^2 / nu)`, so the reported numbers are
   bit-for-bit reproducible.
3. After the flow is statistically steady (drift of the peak velocity over the
   last quarter of the run), the velocity is averaged in time over the last
   window and binned onto the lattice rows. The spread inside a row is the
   measure of how two-dimensional the solution stayed; it is reported as
   `max row std / u_max`.
4. The profile is fitted with `u = c0 + c1 y + c2 y^2` (linear least squares).
   From `c2 = -g_x/(2 nu_eff)` the effective viscosity follows, from
   `c1/c2 = a + b` and `-c0/c2 = a b` the two wall positions follow, and the
   symmetric offset is `delta = (-H + sqrt(H^2 - 4 a b))/2`. Viscosity and wall
   position are therefore read from *different* coefficients: a too-viscous
   operator cannot hide behind a shifted wall.
5. A second fit restricted to the middle half of the channel (`0.25 <= y/H <= 0.75`)
   measures the bulk operator without the wall layer.

### 3.3 Convergence

The peak-velocity error falls from 24.1% at `H/dx = 8` to 1.5% at `H/dx = 16`, i.e. an observed order of
**4.00** over the whole ladder (1.88 then 6.98 between consecutive pairs).

That order is **not** a discretisation order and should not be quoted as one: the two defects have opposite signs in the peak velocity (an offset no-slip plane raises it, the wall slip lowers it), so the residual error is a difference of two first-order effects and its apparent rate is erratic. The wall-shear column, which has no such cancellation, is the honest convergence indicator.

The wall offset `delta/dx` is resolution-independent at
0.91 to 0.91, which is what a first-order wall
boundary condition looks like: a fixed fraction of a spacing, so `delta -> 0`
only as `dx -> 0`.

### 3.4 Negative control: free slip

With `wall_friction = 0` no tangential stress acts at the wall, so nothing
balances the body force and the column accelerates rigidly: `u -> g_x t` with a
flat profile. Measured at `H/dx = 8`, `t = 1.0 s`: mean
`u = 8.870 m/s` against the analytic
`g_x t = 9.810 m/s` (ratio 0.904), row-to-row
spread 0.00% of the mean, and a velocity
still growing by 20.3% over the last quarter of the
run. This is the control that makes the no-slip parabola a measurement rather
than a tautology: the same harness produces a flat, unbounded profile when the
wall condition is switched off.

## 4. Error verdict

The gate is the usual one for this repository: relative error on the key
quantity below 3 %, else the case is not validated.

| key quantity | reference | best measured | relative error | < 3 %? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| peak velocity `u_max` | `g_x H^2/(8 nu) = 1.2263` m/s | 1.2448 m/s (H/dx=16) | 1.5% | **yes** |
| wall shear `tau_w`, 1-`dx` secant (see 4.1) | `rho g_x H/2 = 490.5` Pa | 246.5 Pa | -49.7% | **no** |
| pointwise profile, interior | analytic parabola | 8.51% RMS | 8.51% | **no** |
| effective bulk viscosity | `nu = 0.01` | 1.149 nu (middle-half fit) | 14.9% | **no** |
| no-slip at the wall | `u(0) = 0` | 31.4% of `u_max` | — | **no** |

**Answer: it depends which quantity, and the honest summary is 'not validated'.** The peak velocity is inside 3 % at `H/dx = 16` (1.5%), but the wall shear is -50% off at that resolution and stays there across the ladder (42%, 37%, 50% off at `H/dx = 8, 12, 16`).
The last row is a *one-spacing secant* and it is not the drag the wall transmits: measured from the momentum budget of the same run the wall removes `D/(M g) = 1.0091` of the injected streamwise momentum at `H/dx = 16` (0.91%), i.e. the missing gradient is a property of the stencil and of the unresolved wall layer, not of the wall model. The audit, its calibration control and the withdrawn sentence are in 4.1 and in `docs/verification_poiseuille_wall_shear_estimator.md`.

A momentum balance that a steady channel must satisfy pointwise is still
violated by half *as a gradient reading*, so the peak agreement cannot be
presented as the wall being right. The apparent order of the peak error,
4.00, is a difference of two first-order defects and is not evidence
of a converging no-slip condition.

### 4.1 What the -50 % wall-shear reading measures

Three independent readings of the same shear, from the same stored run (`benchmarks/poiseuille/wall_shear_estimators.py`, artifact `out/wall_shear_estimators.json`):

| `H/dx` | 1-`dx` secant | same secant on the *exact* parabola | global parabola slope at the wall | drag from the momentum budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.580 | 0.750 | 0.820 | 0.989 |
| 12 | 0.630 | 0.833 | 0.828 | 1.003 |
| 16 | 0.503 | 0.875 | 0.812 | 1.009 |
| analytic | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

The free-slip control calibrates the drift estimator to 0.45% against its exact answer `d<u>/dt = g`, so the budget column is good to about a percent; the worst deviation over the ladder and over two fit windows is 1.05% / 1.49%. Two things follow. (i) The wall transmits the full momentum flux, to within the estimator's resolution -- `a steady channel cannot do otherwise` -- so the secant reading above is a gradient bias, not a drag deficit. (ii) Of the -49.7% secant bias at `H/dx = 16`, -12.5% is the stencil's own geometry (`1 - 2 dx/H`) and the rest is the shape of the smeared wall layer, which puts the first fluid row at 31.4% of `u_max`.

What *is* verified:

* the discrete viscous stress holds a shear against a constant load: the
  measured profile is a parabola with residual 1.2% of `u_max`;
* the driving force is applied correctly and nothing else balances it: the
  free-slip control reproduces the rigid-body analytic `u = g_x t` to 9.6%;
* the flow stays *nearly* two-dimensional, but not exactly: the standard
  deviation of `u_x` inside a lattice row peaks at 8.1% of `u_max` (in the wall rows) and is
  under 1 % in the middle rows. See the caveat in section 5.
* the bulk effective viscosity of the operator is within
  14.9% of 1 in the middle
  half of the channel, i.e. the operator itself is calibrated to better than the
  wall model it is attached to.

What is *not* verified, and why:

* the no-slip condition. The `wall_friction` blend applies viscosity between
  fluid particles and *static wall particles one spacing outside the boundary*,
  so the effective no-slip plane sits at about `+0.9 dx`
  at every resolution (a fixed fraction of a spacing, hence `delta -> 0` only as
  `dx -> 0`), and the fluid slips at the wall instead of stopping. That is the
  source of both the low shear and the high peak, and it is a property of the
  boundary *formulation*, not of the viscous operator.
* whether the parabolic shape itself is a resolution artifact: every resolution
  produces a parabola (residual under 2 % of `u_max`), including the coarse one
  whose wall layer spans a fifth of the channel, so the parabola is the shape the
  scheme locks onto rather than a strict consequence of the wall condition.

## 5. Notes, traps and scope

* **Snapshots are not independent.** With a fixed `dt` the start-up transient
  is deterministic; a run that stops too early reports a profile that is still
  steepening. The `steady drift` column is the guard: it must be well under 1 %
  before any row value is quoted (measured 1.08% here).
* **Thread count changes the numbers only through the clock.** With the default
  torch CPU thread pool these tensors are so small that thread overhead
  dominates: the same step costs 13x more wall clock with 8 threads than with 1.
  The numbers are identical; the benchmark pins `torch.set_num_threads(1)`.
* **The body force is applied outside `rhs`**, because `rhs` only knows a
  `-y` gravity. The Adami wall-pressure extrapolation therefore carries no
  body-force term along `x`; for a field that is linear in `x` the kernel
  average reproduces the wall value to second order, so this is a small
  contribution to the wall defect, not its cause.
* **The residual x-scatter is acoustic, and it lives at the wall.** The field
  figure shows rows that are uniform to well under 1 % of `u_max` in the middle
  of the channel and rows with up to 8% scatter
  next to the walls. At Mach 0.07 the pressure waves from the wall-pressure
  extrapolation are not negligible, which is another reason not to read the peak
  velocity agreement as a converged boundary condition.
* **2-D.** This is a plane channel, not a pipe. The `1/8` factor and the wall
  shear `1/2` are the plane-Poiseuille values; a circular pipe has `1/16` and
  `1/4`.
* The free-slip control reaches Mach 0.49 by the end of
  the window; it is used only for its *flatness*, which is a kinematic
  statement, not a compressible-flow result.

## 6. Consequence for the solver

`docs/algorithm-gap-analysis.md` leaves the default `wall_friction` open pending
a comparison like this one. The measurement says: at the resolutions this solver
is used at for a 0.1 m channel (`H/dx = 8 .. 16`), the blended no-slip condition
produces a slip velocity of 49% to 31% of the centre-line speed, and the
near-wall gradient a 1-`dx` secant can recover from it is 58% to 50% of
`rho g H/2` -- the drag itself is transmitted in full (4.1), but the profile
inside one spacing of the wall is not resolved well enough to read it as a
stress. Concrete consequences:

* keep `wall_friction = 0` (free slip) as the default, as it is now. A no-slip
  default would add a boundary layer the solver cannot resolve, and any wall
  shear read from its gradient would be 40-50 % low at `H/dx = 8..16` (4.1).
* do not attach a no-slip claim to a result obtained with `wall_friction = 1`
  unless the wall layer is resolved much finer than `H/dx = 16`; the peak
  velocity may look right because the errors cancel.
* if no-slip matters for the physics (wall shear stress, boundary-layer growth,
  lubrication), replace the blend with mirrored/ghost particles that impose
  `u = 0` at the boundary instead of dragging the fluid towards it. This
  benchmark is the acceptance test for that replacement: the wall-shear ratio
  must reach 1 within 3 %.
