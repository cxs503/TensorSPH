## 2. Why it belongs in TensorSPH

`benchmarks/capillary_wave/` shipped a dispersion/damping harness against Lamb
(1932) but committed no evidence, so the ledger carried it as evidence debt E1
("harness only, no committed artifact").  A capability that cannot produce a
gradable reading must say so with numbers, not with silence — that is the same
contract `claim_validity/audit.json` and `laplace_pressure_closure/verdict_matrix.json`
already follow in this repository.

## 5. Notes, limits

1. **Not a pass.** The verdict stays WITHHELD: at HEAD a linear capillary
   standing wave is destroyed within two periods by the discrete CSF force on a
   one-sided (open) free surface, and no knob (density renormalisation, mass
   calibration, both donor pressure closures, legacy CSF, square lattice, higher
   sound speed, larger amplitude) brings the spurious current inside the Adami
   et al. (2012) 10 % limit.
2. **Scope.** Closed interfaces are out of scope here: the Young-Laplace drop
   ladder and the two-phase control behave differently, so the failure is
   specific to the open support, not to the measuring chain.
3. **Full CI suite not completed locally.** The suite is 1427 tests; the heavy
   SPH cases (30 files, e.g. `test_adaptive_dt.py`, `test_convergence.py`,
   `test_dam_break.py`, `test_oscillating_droplet.py`) exceed the single-call
   budget of this working environment.  They were run on `main` as well and are
   untouched by this change: it adds no `src/` file and no shared module.
4. **Numerical reproducibility is exact, wall clock is not.** Timing fields are
   bookkeeping; the audit records them per variant, so a load-dependent change is
   visible but never mistaken for a physics change.
